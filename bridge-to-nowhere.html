Backpacking / Day Hike — San Gabriel Mountains

Bridge to Nowhere

Ten miles up a river canyon to a bridge that never got to be a road, and back. Mostly flat, mostly in the water.

The trail follows the East Fork of the San Gabriel River upstream through a steep canyon, crossing the river repeatedly on the way to a striking concrete arch bridge stranded in the middle of the wilderness. It's flat and doesn't gain much elevation, but the constant river crossings and rocky footing make it a longer day than the mileage suggests.

The story

The bridge was built in 1936 as part of a planned road connecting the San Gabriel Valley to Wrightwood. A major flood in 1938 wiped out the road before it was ever finished, leaving the bridge stranded with no road on either side — hence the name. It's now one of the more distinctive landmarks in the San Gabriels, and on weekends draws a crowd, including permitted bungee jumpers off the span itself.

The route

Starting from the Coyote Flat trailhead at the end of East Fork Road, the trail stays close to the riverbed the whole way in, crossing back and forth across the East Fork multiple times. Expect wet feet — river shoes or quick-drying boots make a real difference here. About two and a half miles in, the trail crosses into the Sheep Mountain Wilderness, where old fragments of the abandoned road are still visible underfoot.

Permit: A free wilderness permit is required inside Sheep Mountain Wilderness — self-issue at the trailhead kiosk. An Adventure Pass is also needed to park at the trailhead.
Conditions: Wildfire has affected this canyon in recent years, and both trail conditions and river crossings can change season to season. Check current Angeles National Forest closures before heading out, especially after storms.

Trailhead

East Fork Road, off Highway 39 (San Gabriel Canyon Road) north of Azusa — roughly an hour and fifteen minutes from campus. The trail starts at the Coyote Flat parking lot, past a large yellow gate.

Want to lead this one?

A good option for a mixed-experience group — bring extra socks and check trail conditions the week of.
