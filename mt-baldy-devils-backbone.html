Hiking — San Gabriel Mountains

Mt. Baldy via
Devil's Backbone

The long way up the tallest peak in the San Gabriels — a ridge-top trail with the best views on the mountain, and no easy way to reach them.

Devil's Backbone is the longer, more exposed way up Mt. Baldy (officially Mt. San Antonio), the highest point in the San Gabriel Mountains. Instead of the steeper, more direct Baldy Bowl route, this trail climbs a narrow, wind-scoured ridge with drop-offs on both sides — less of a straight grind, more of a mountain with a bit of exposure to keep things interesting.

The route

Start at Manker Flats and follow the dirt service road up toward the Mt. Baldy ski lift, passing San Antonio Falls along the way. From Baldy Notch, the trail narrows onto the Devil's Backbone itself — a ridgeline with sweeping views down into both San Antonio Canyon and the desert side of the range. After crossing the backbone, the trail climbs a final steep push to the summit, where on a clear day you can see from the Mojave to the ocean.

Most groups do it as an out-and-back, but some leaders route it as a loop, descending via the steeper Baldy Bowl trail instead of retracing the Backbone.

What to bring

This is a full-day mountain trip, not a casual hike. Bring more water than you think you need, real food (not just snacks), sun protection, and layers — it's routinely 20–30 degrees colder and significantly windier at the summit than at the trailhead. An Adventure Pass is required to park at Manker Flats.

Winter conditions: Snow and ice are common on the Backbone from late fall through spring, and the exposed sections can turn genuinely dangerous without microspikes or crampons and the experience to use them. OTL only runs this as a winter trip with leaders trained for it — bring your own traction gear.

Trailhead

Manker Flats, off Mt. Baldy Road above the village of Mt. Baldy — about an hour from campus. Parking fills early on weekends, so an OTL trip usually means a pre-dawn carpool.
